# Concept 1 — engineered tank and modular cathodes

## Use case

Compare this route when Version 1/2 site verification confirms the same long process tank and inspection identifies a cathode-support, current distribution, containment, liner, or serviceability issue.

## Arrangement

- Keep cathode support loads on calculated top seats/cross-frame or an independent structural frame, not on an unverified liner wall.
- Design cathodes as removable modules with maintainable electrical contacts and safe isolation. Material and position depend on the confirmed bath and quality specification.
- Preserve an unobstructed work rack/hoist envelope and avoid obstructing flow, cooling, cleaning, or local exhaust.
- For a new vessel, compare shop-welded PP, site-assembled PP, liner in structural shell, dual containment and dual-laminate options from [`pp-tank/`](../../../../pp-tank/README.md).
- For a retrofit, characterize material, dimensions, welds, supports, wall movement, base and containment before deciding the vessel can remain.

## Why it is an international-grade design practice

The advance is engineered load paths, modular maintenance, verified electrical connection and containment/integrity documentation—not an assumed “foreign tank” shape. Tank materials and dimensions must follow the exact bath, temperature, liquid head, design life and local tank standards. HSE PM86 describes integrity management and competent inspection for thermoplastic tanks.

## Alternatives

Top-suspended cathodes; removable crossbeam; independent floor-supported frame; designed polymer cleats; PP liner plus structural shell; new PP/dual-laminate vessel. Wall-penetrating brackets are only an engineered option if leak-path and structural checks accept them.

## Required verification

Bath identity, polarity, work area/load, cathode area and spacing, current profile, joint voltage drop/temperature, hoist clearance, tank support calculation, polymer compatibility, qualified fabrication and leak-test plan. Do not size from the media.
