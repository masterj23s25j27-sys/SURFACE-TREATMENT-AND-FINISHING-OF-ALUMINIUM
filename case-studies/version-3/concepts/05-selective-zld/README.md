# Concept 5 — selective near-ZLD / closed-loop water strategy

## What it means

Near-ZLD is a system-level water strategy that first reduces demand and segregates streams, recovers reusable chemistry, reuses suitable permeate, and concentrates a smaller purge through membranes and/or evaporation. A crystallizer may be appropriate only if the salt/residual outlet, energy and economics are solved.

## Recommended hierarchy

1. Prevent leakage and unnecessary water use.
2. Improve drain-back and rinse layout.
3. Recover suitable process chemicals and reuse suitable rinse water.
4. Treat residual streams to the permit and reuse specification.
5. Concentrate only a characterized final purge if required; identify final brine/salt/sludge destination.

## Why not assume ZLD is best

EU BAT documentation notes that zero discharge can be achieved in particular cases but is not generally BAT for a whole installation. High recovery may shift pollutants into concentrate or salt, increase heat/electricity demand, accelerate scaling/corrosion, and create a difficult residual. “No liquid outfall” does not mean no waste. Compare the whole water-energy-chemical-residual balance and regulatory route.

## Feasibility study requirements

Seasonal water balance, reuse demand, feed characterization, water quality requirements by process stage, pilot recovery/fouling data, energy/heat integration, reliability and redundancy, concentrate chemistry, waste acceptance contract, capex/opex, outage, and life-cycle emissions/cost. Proceed only with environmental/regulator and process-quality approvals.
