# Version 4 Installation Planning Guide

## Purpose and limits

This guide organizes the engineering approvals, site preparation, installation coordination, and handover records for the Version 4 cathode and jig concepts. It is a **planning guide**, not a fabrication drawing, electrical wiring instruction, chemical-handling procedure, lift plan, or rectifier operating procedure. Work on the tank and rectifier must follow the approved site method statements, permits, isolation rules, and drawings prepared by qualified engineers and contractors.

Do not start installation from the concept images alone. The actual bath chemistry, tank condition, cathode area, rack dimensions, support loads, rectifier topology, and production recipe have not been verified by this study.

## Design basis to confirm at site

| Parameter | Current planning value | Confirmation required before installation |
|---|---:|---|
| Tank length | 17.5 ft | Measure clear internal length; record liner, fittings, repairs, and support locations. |
| Tank width | 2.5 ft | Confirm internal clear width and the rack/cathode movement envelope. |
| Normal liquid depth | 2.5 ft | Confirm actual working level and tank maker's required freeboard. |
| Tank depth | 3 ft | Verify tank drawing and condition. |
| Capacity | 3,000 L stated | The unobstructed rectangular envelope at the stated liquid depth calculates to about 3,098 L. Confirm capacity with a calibrated level/volume table and account for displacement. |
| Rectifier alternatives | 24 V / 2,000 A or 30 V / 1,000 A | Confirm nameplate, continuous-duty rating, output behavior, protections, cooling, supply, current/voltage measurement, and approved connection drawing. Do not combine the alternatives based on this guide. |
| Process | Aluminum surface treatment/anodizing context in prior records | Confirm exact process, bath composition, temperature, work alloy, customer specification, and polarity from the plant's controlled records. |

## Required project package

The installation owner should have the following documents approved and available at the work area:

- Issued-for-construction tank, cathode support, bus, rack/jig, lifting-interface, and layout drawings with revision numbers.
- Tank material and inspection records; support and foundation review; containment and leak-control plan.
- Process specification and approved work-area/cathode-area calculations for each representative load family.
- Electrical single-line/interface drawing, equipment datasheets, protection and isolation design, cable/bus schedule, and commissioning method from the plant electrical engineer.
- Structural calculations for supports, anchors, lifted assemblies, wet load, and handling loads.
- Contractor method statement, job hazard analysis, task permits, isolation/lockout plan, lifting plan, ventilation/EHS review, and emergency arrangements.
- Inspection and acceptance forms for fit-up, electrical checks, coating qualification, operator training, and maintenance handover.

## Roles and release gates

| Gate | Owner(s) | Evidence required to proceed |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Process and tank basis | Process owner, tank fabricator, production | Bath/process identity, verified tank dimensions/material/condition, load envelope, controlled specification. |
| 2. Design release | Structural/tank engineer, electrical engineer, process engineer, EHS | Signed drawings and calculations, approved support/load path, electrical design, compatibility and risk reviews. |
| 3. Work readiness | Installation lead, maintenance, EHS, contractors | Site permits, isolation plan, tank preparation, lifting/access plan, barriers, ventilation, emergency provisions, and authorized crew brief. |
| 4. Mechanical acceptance | Tank fabricator/structural engineer, installation lead | Fit-up, support seating, restraint, alignment, clearance, lift-interface, and vessel/containment inspection records. |
| 5. Electrical and process release | Qualified electrician, electrical/process owners, quality | Approved inspection records, verified isolation/protection/polarity under the site test method, calibrated instruments, and signed commissioning authorization. |
| 6. Production handover | Process owner, quality, maintenance, EHS | Approved trial results, operating limits entered into controlled procedures, training, maintenance plan, and complete as-built package. |

Do not bypass a gate because a concept sketch appears to fit. Record deviations and obtain written review from the responsible discipline before work continues.

## Controlled installation sequence

### 1. Survey and freeze the design basis

1. Walk down the installation against the Version 1 survey checklist and record measured internal dimensions, bath level, tank supports, liner/weld condition, containment, services, cooling, ventilation, access, and hoist travel.
2. Confirm the process and part families. Record the largest work/rack envelope, wet mass, exposed work area, approved contact zones, and representative load patterns.
3. Confirm the selected rectifier and obtain its controlled datasheet, duty rating, output/protection details, and approved connection drawings. Have the electrical owner confirm actual plant polarity and measurement points.
4. Close the design gates in the two Version 4 design packages. Issue a controlled drawing set and installation method statement before procurement or fabrication.

### 2. Prepare and isolate the work area

1. Schedule the outage and appoint one installation lead with authority to stop work.
2. Have trained site personnel prepare the tank under the site's approved chemical removal, cleaning, and isolation procedure. Verify and document the safe work condition before mechanical work begins.
3. Apply the facility's electrical and process isolation/lockout procedure to the rectifier, buswork, pumps, heaters, hoist interfaces, and other relevant services. Verify isolation using the site's approved method and qualified staff.
4. Establish barriers, access control, ventilation, spill/emergency arrangements, lifting exclusion zones, and any required permits. Tank entry is outside this guide; if required, follow the site's confined-space program and entry authorization.

### 3. Install the mechanical support and dry-fit components

1. Inspect the tank and support points against the approved drawings. Stop if dimensions, damage, deflection, or support details do not match the design basis.
2. Install the engineered rim saddles, bridge, integrated seats, or independent frame using the approved lift plan and installation method. Keep structural loads on the approved load path; do not improvise wetted-wall holes, brackets, or tank attachments.
3. With the rectifier isolated, dry-fit removable cathode modules and representative unloaded rack/jig components. Confirm alignment, retention, maintenance access, lift balance, travel envelope, freeboard, and clearance from tank fittings, coils, and other conductive parts.
4. Check the complete hoist/rack path and any planned load sway against the approved minimum clearances. Document measured results on the mechanical acceptance form.
5. Remove components as required for final work and confirm the vessel/containment inspection specified by the tank engineer before returning the tank to service.

### 4. Complete electrical interfaces

1. A qualified electrician completes the rectifier, bus, protective devices, isolation, guarding, and bonding interfaces only to the approved electrical drawings and applicable site rules.
2. Keep electrical joints in the locations and environment defined by the approved design. Protect the connections from bath splash and mist as specified by the design owner.
3. Do not use the concept polarity diagram as a wiring instruction. The electrical and process owners must independently verify the correct connections, isolation points, protection coordination, instruments, and test method.
4. Record equipment identifiers, as-built conductor/bus routing, inspection results, instrument calibration status, and deviations. Any unapproved mismatch stops release.

### 5. Inspect, qualify, and authorize commissioning

1. Complete the tank, support, restraint, lifting-interface, guarding, clearance, containment, and electrical inspection forms while the system remains in the approved safe state.
2. Correct all defects and close deviations through documented engineering review. Confirm that every interlock, alarm, protective device, emergency control, and local procedure has an identified owner and test record.
3. The process owner prepares a separate controlled commissioning procedure with approved operating limits, trial load, environmental controls, sampling/quality plan, stop criteria, and responsible operators.
4. Begin any bath filling, energized testing, or production trial only after written authorization under site procedures. This guide does not specify energization, ramp rates, setpoints, chemical additions, or live troubleshooting steps.
5. Qualify representative loads and record the approved process data, coating results, contact condition, current/voltage traces, temperature profile, and any required environmental measurements. Obtain production release from process and quality owners.

## Handover checklist

- [ ] Approved and as-built tank, cathode, jig/rack, support, lift-interface, and electrical drawings archived.
- [ ] Tank inspection, support calculations, containment records, and deviations closed.
- [ ] Rectifier datasheet, approved connection design, protection checks, instrument calibration, and electrical acceptance records archived.
- [ ] Cathode material/area/position and jig/load patterns recorded for each qualified product family.
- [ ] Representative-load qualification and coating quality acceptance signed by process and quality owners.
- [ ] Approved controlled operating and emergency procedures issued by the plant; this planning guide is not a substitute.
- [ ] Operators and maintenance staff trained on the released configuration, isolation, inspection, and stop-work conditions.
- [ ] Maintenance plan covers cathodes, contacts, supports, insulation, electrical joints, hoist interfaces, tank integrity, containment, and sensors.
- [ ] Version numbers, revision owners, source data, commissioning evidence, and review dates recorded.
